PA

Site & Environmental Analysis

Solar & orientation

The best overall orientation in the northern hemisphere is the principal facade facing south or 5–25° east of south — maximizing winter passive solar gain while limiting east/west summer heat gain. Altitude is the sun's angle above the horizon; azimuth is its horizontal angle measured from due south. The sun is lowest at the winter solstice and highest at the summer solstice, rising/setting due east-west at the equinoxes.

Shading: horizontal overhangs work on south glass (block high summer sun, admit low winter sun); vertical fins work on east/west facades (low morning/afternoon sun). Exterior shading always beats interior because it stops the sun before it reaches the glass. The solar envelope is the 3D volume a building must stay within so it does not shadow neighbors.

Climate & wind

Four U.S. climate regions: cool (maximize solar gain, compact form, windbreaks); temperate (flexible/mixed); hot-humid (maximize ventilation and shade, elevate, minimize E/W exposure); hot-arid (heavy thermal mass, small windows, evaporative cooling, shade courts). Energy-code compliance uses IECC/ASHRAE 90.1 climate zones for R-values and U-factors.

Topography & legal constraints

Slope = (rise/run) × 100. 0–5% level; 5–10% buildable with grading; 10–25% steep (switchbacks, erosion); over 25% generally not buildable. Easements are recorded rights to use/restrict land (utility, access, support, joint-use, scenic). Deed restrictions (restrictive covenants) are private deed limitations binding future owners. A setback is measured from the right-of-way line, not the street centerline.

Zoning, Codes & Regulations

Codes operate at three levels: federal (ADA is civil-rights law enforced through the 2010 ADA Standards; the IBC, not the ADA, references ICC/ANSI A117.1), state (adopts the IBC with amendments; each state decides independently when to adopt — no federal timeline), and local (zoning controls use, density, height, setbacks, FAR, parking). FAR = total floor area (all stories) / site area; BCR = footprint / site area × 100.

Code conflicts: always default to the most stringent requirement. Zoning allows 5 stories but the building code 8? The limit is 5.

Programming & Area

Organization & circulation

Six organization concepts: linear, axial, grid, central, radial, clustered. Linear/axial/grid favor structural and mechanical economy; central/radial are formal and costlier; clustered adapts to varied space sizes. Establish circulation first as the organizing element — letting adjacencies drive the plan and adding corridors later creates maze-like layouts and egress problems. Programming translates client needs into a quantified space list via bubble diagrams, then a conceptual budget.

Area definitions

Go RUN (most to least inclusive): Gross, Rentable, Usable, Net. Net-to-gross (Net/Gross) is always <1.0 (warehouse ~0.95, classroom building ~0.65). Grossing factor (Gross/Net) is always >1.0.

Code Analysis: Occupancy, Type, Height & Area

The IBC sequence: (1) occupancy classification (usually fixed by program) → (2) construction type (usually the architect's choice) → (3) allowable height and area from the combination plus sprinkler status.

Occupancy groups: A Assembly, B Business, E Educational, F Factory, H High-Hazard, I Institutional, M Mercantile, R Residential, S Storage, U Utility. Five construction types I–V: I and II noncombustible (I = most fire-resistive), III/IV/V combustible (IV = Heavy Timber, V-B = unprotected wood frame).

Area increases: sprinkler increases are built into Table 506.2 (single-story S1 = 4× NS; multistory SM = 3× NS per story); frontage on a public way/open space adds up to +75%. Total building area ≈ per-story allowable × number of stories (max 3).

Egress & Accessibility

Every egress path has three parts: exit access, exit (fire-rated), and exit discharge. Exits required: 1–49=1, 50–500=2, 501–1000=3, 1000+=4, separated by ½ the longest diagonal (⅓ if sprinklered). Stair width = occupants ×0.3, or ×0.2 where the building is sprinklered and has an emergency voice/alarm communication system; min 44 in.

Accessibility derives from a wheelchair user's clearances: 36 in continuous route, 32 in clear at a doorway, 60 in turning circle, ramp max slope 1:12. Any route steeper than 1:20 is a ramp. Interior door force max 5 lbf; lever hardware (no round knobs).

PPD

HVAC Systems

Systems are grouped by transport medium. DX (self-contained, single rooms). All-air: constant-volume single duct (cannot zone), VAV (varies air volume per zone, large open offices), dual-duct (hot+cool mixed, high-rise, costly), reheat (labs/hospitals, energy-intensive), multizone. All-water (hydronic): two-pipe (hot OR chilled, seasonal changeover) vs four-pipe (simultaneous heat and cool). Heat pumps move heat; geothermal uses stable ground temperature and excels in extreme climates. Economizers give free cooling (air-side uses outdoor air directly; water-side cools the chilled loop via the tower). A zone shares one thermostat — zone by exposure, schedule, or air-quality needs.

Thermal Envelope & Psychrometrics

Six related properties: conductivity k, resistivity r=1/k, conductance C=k/d, resistance R=1/C (layers add in series), transmittance U=1/ΣR (whole assembly; what codes regulate; lower=better). Heat flow: Q = U·A·ΔT (conduction) and Q = 1.08·CFM·ΔT (infiltration).

Psychrometrics: dry-bulb (air temp), wet-bulb (evaporatively cooled — the spread indicates RH), dew point (condensation temp), enthalpy (total heat = sensible + latent), sensible (changes temperature), latent (phase change, no temperature change).

Vapor control: place the vapor retarder on the warm side of the insulation (interior in cold climates, exterior in hot-humid). Continuous insulation outboard of framing eliminates thermal bridging. Never trap insulation between two vapor retarders.

Electrical & Lighting

Electrical basics

Voltage (pressure), current (flow), resistance (opposition), power P=V×I (DC). Distribution: utility → service entrance → main panel → subpanels → branch circuits. Common U.S. voltages: 120V outlets, 208/240V appliances, 277V commercial lighting, 480V (3-phase) large motors/HVAC. Breakers are sized to the conductor (wire), not the load. GFCI in wet areas; AFCI in living spaces.

Lighting

Lumens = output; foot-candles = light reaching a surface; efficacy = lumens/watt. CRI (color accuracy 0–100, keep >80) is independent of CCT (apparent warmth; counterintuitively HIGH K = cool/blue, LOW K = warm). Max adjacent luminance ratio 10:1. A light shelf bounces daylight deeper into a room. LED dominates new work; HPS is efficient but renders color poorly.

Structures & Seismic

Loads & systems

Loads: gravity (dead = self-weight; live = occupancy), lateral (wind and seismic), and miscellaneous. Wind creates inward pressure and outward suction (suction can exceed pressure). Lateral-force-resisting systems by stiffness: shear walls >> braced frames >> moment frames (moment frames give the most open plan but deflect most). Foundations: spread footings (good near-grade soil) vs deep piles/caissons (carry load through poor soil to bedrock).

Seismic

Inertial force F = M×A, so lighter buildings attract less force. Resonance amplifies response when a building's natural period matches ground motion (soft soils amplify and lengthen it). Ductility lets a structure bend before breaking. Diaphragms transfer lateral load via chords (edges) and collectors. Codes require strong column / weak beam.

Worst configuration: the soft/weak story — an open, flexible ground floor under stiffer upper floors. Other hazards: discontinuous shear walls, torsional irregularity, and reentrant corners (L/T/U plans).

Building Envelope & Moisture

Four control layers: water-resistive barrier (sheds bulk water), air barrier (stops air-carried heat/moisture; continuous; can be vapor-permeable — often higher impact than added insulation), thermal layer, and cladding. Wall types: mass wall, cavity/veneer (drainage cavity + weep holes), curtain wall (non-load-bearing, needs thermal breaks), EIFS (drainage-plane "barrier EIFS" now required). The rain-screen principle separates cladding from the WRB with a vented cavity. Windows: lower U-factor = better insulation; SHGC high for passive gain in cold climates, low to cut cooling in hot climates.

Acoustics

STC rates airborne isolation (50 = loud speech inaudible); IIC rates impact/footfall sound; NRC rates absorption; RT60 = 0.049 V / A is reverberation time (long for music, short for speech); NC sets background-noise targets (HVAC is the dominant source). Isolation = mass + decoupling + cavity absorption (mass law: doubling mass ≈ +6 dB). Flanking (sound through structure) makes field STC fall below lab values. +10 dB ≈ perceived doubling of loudness.

Fire Protection

Three objectives: contain (compartmentation, fire-rated assemblies), suppress (sprinklers/standpipes), detect (alarms). A high-rise has an occupied floor >75 ft above fire-department access — requiring sprinklers, stair pressurization, and smoke control. Smoke control is passive (barriers, curtain boards/draft stops) or active (fans: exhaust the fire zone, pressurize refuge zones/stairs).

NFPA 13 sprinkler types: wet pipe (most common), dry pipe (freeze-prone), pre-action (water-damage-sensitive), deluge (high-hazard, all heads open at once).

Sustainability & Commissioning

Rating systems & metrics

LEED (USGBC): Certified / Silver / Gold / Platinum. Others: BREEAM (first system, UK), Green Globes, Living Building Challenge (net-positive, measured performance), WELL (human health), Passive House (ultra-low energy). EUI (kBtu/sf/yr) normalizes energy use. Net-zero design sequence: reduce loads first, then efficient systems, then renewables. Integrated design engages all consultants from inception (e.g., better windows downsize HVAC, lowering net cost).

Commissioning & POE

Three stages: determine requirements (OPR = what, BOD = how), plan the process, and functional testing (startup, operator training, commissioning report) — ideally re-tested one year post-occupancy. Fundamental commissioning is a LEED prerequisite. A post-occupancy evaluation is an additional service added via AIA G802 (which avoids resetting the statute-of-limitations clock).

Drawings & the project manual

Drawing types include site, floor, roof, reflected ceiling (RCP), elevations, building sections, and wall sections. Wood & Roofing Materials

Wood

Fiber saturation point ~30% MC (below it, wood shrinks and gains strength); structural lumber MC ≤19%, finish ≤15%. Pressure-treat wood near grade/soil or in contact with ground concrete. Engineered products: glulam (long/curved beams), CLT (mass-timber panels), LVL (beams/headers), PSL (posts/beams), wood I-joist. A nominal 2×4 is actually 1.5×3.5 in. Platform framing firestops inherently; balloon framing needs firestops in continuous voids.

Roofing

Steep-slope (≥3:12) sheds water by gravity; low-slope (<3:12) needs a continuous membrane (BUR/modified bitumen, single-ply, fluid-applied). Insulation: below deck (vented), above deck (unvented "hot roof"), or above membrane (inverted/IRMA, closed-cell). Cool roofs combine albedo and emissivity (→ SRI). Attic ventilation 1/150 of floor area (1/300 with vapor retarder).
